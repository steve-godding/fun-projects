Four things to set up before the shooting starts

All on the iPhone, all one-offs. The third one is the whole system — everything later hangs off that album existing.

  • Check iCloud has room for the trip

    Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage. Sixteen days of a couple's photos is realistically 10–25 GB — a still is 2–4 MB, but 4K video runs 200–400 MB per minute and that's what fills a plan. If you're near the top of 50 GB, move to 200 GB now. A phone that stops backing up mid-cruise is how a trip gets lost.

  • Confirm iCloud Photos is on — this is your backup

    Settings → Photos → Sync this iPhone. With it on, every shot is off the phone within minutes of finding WiFi, so a phone left in a taxi in Kagoshima costs you a phone and not the holiday. Also set Optimise iPhone Storage so the phone can't fill up.

    While you're there, turn Cellular Data off for Photos. On a roaming eSIM you want uploads happening on hotel and ship WiFi, not on data you're paying for by the megabyte.

  • Make one empty album called Japan 2026

    Albums tab → + → New Album. Leave it empty. It is the bucket the nightly habit pours into, and the thing you'll hand to Kim, Charles and Shirley and your family later. One album for the whole trip — don't make one per port, you'll stop doing it by Busan.

  • Decide who sees what, once

    Four different mechanisms, and picking the wrong one is the usual mess. The next section lays them out — the short version is Shared Library for Kim, Shared Album for everyone else.

Every night, before you turn the light off

The two-minute habit that does the whole job

This is the entire cull, spread across sixteen small painless pieces instead of one four-hour job in October that you will not do. Order matters — heart before you delete.

  1. Scroll back through today 30 sec

    Open Photos and scroll to this morning. Don't judge anything yet, just look — you're reminding yourself what the day actually contained.

  2. Heart the keepers 60 sec

    Tap the ♡ on the ten or fifteen shots you'd show someone. That's it — that's the cull. In three weeks you won't be sorting three thousand photos, you'll be sorting the four hundred you hearted, and past-you already did the hard part while you still remembered which of the five torii shots was the one where the light was right.

  3. Bin the obvious rubbish 20 sec

    Screenshots, blurry ones, the accidental shot of your shoe, the four identical menu photos. Delete them now, while it's obvious. They go to Recently Deleted and sit there for 30 days, so nothing is final.

  4. Add the day's keepers to Japan 2026 20 sec

    Tap Select, then drag your finger across today's keepers to grab them in one go — you don't have to tap each one. Then Add to Album → Japan 2026.

  5. Caption the two you'll forget 10 sec

    Swipe up on a photo and type into Add a Caption. "Sengan-en, Kagoshima" takes four seconds now and is unrecoverable in 2031. Captions are searchable, so this is also how you find things later. Two a day is plenty.

Sharing

Four mechanisms, and the one job each is good at

Apple gives these near-identical names and they behave completely differently. Read the catches — the first two are the ones that surprise people.

iCloud Shared Photo LibraryFor Kim
A second library that you and Kim both contribute to and both fully own — her shots and yours land in one pool, on both phones, at full quality. Set it up once (Settings → Photos → Shared Library) and you can have it add automatically when you're photographing together, so neither of you has to send the other anything all trip.
Catch: it's a shared library, not a copy. If Kim deletes a photo from it, it's gone for you too. Up to 5 people, so don't add the other couple here.
Shared AlbumFor everyone else
The one to use for Charles and Shirley and for family at home. It produces a public web link that works on Android, Windows, anything — no Apple account needed to look. People can like, comment, and add their own shots of the same day, which is how you get their photos of you.
Catch: photos are downsized to about 2048px on the long edge and video is compressed. Perfect for looking at, useless for printing — so this is a sharing tool, never your backup.
Copy iCloud LinkOne-off sends
Select some photos, Share → Copy iCloud Link, paste into any message. Sends them at full original quality, which the shared album won't. This is the right tool for "here are the forty best, at proper resolution" once you're home.
Catch: the link expires after about 30 days, so tell people to save what they want.
AirDropWhile you're standing there
Full quality, instant, no internet or data required — which matters a lot on a ship. Use it to pull the other couple's photos of the two of you on the day, at dinner, rather than chasing them by email in November when they've stopped caring.
Catch: only works between Apple devices, in the same room.
Back in Perth, on the Mac

Twenty minutes to the album people actually watch

All of this is easier on the Mac than the phone. If you did the nightly habit, steps 1 and 2 take a couple of minutes.

  1. Catch anything that missed the album

    File → New Smart Album, set Date Captured is in the range 2 Sep 2026 to 16 Sep 2026. That is the entire trip, from every device signed in, with no dragging — a safety net under the nightly habit.

  2. Merge the duplicates

    In the sidebar under Utilities → Duplicates, Photos has already found the exact and near-identical pairs — including the ones that arrive twice via AirDrop and shared albums. Merge keeps the best version and combines the metadata. It's safe, and it's the single biggest count reduction you'll get.

  3. Cut the favourites down to a best-of

    Filter Japan 2026 to Favourites and make a second album, Japan 2026 — Best Of. Aim for about five per day, so eighty all up. That's the ruthless bit and it's the bit that matters: eighty photos get watched to the end, four hundred get abandoned at Kobe.

  4. Look at it on the map

    The Places view draws the whole trip on a map of Japan and Korea from the GPS in each shot. It's the best way to remember which headland was which — and it's a good check that a day isn't missing.

  5. Let Photos make the movie, then fix it

    Photos will offer a Memory for the trip with music. Don't accept its selection — open it, and swap its picks for your best-of. A four-minute version is the thing you'll actually put on the TV when people come over.

Five things that bite people

  • Deleting from a shared library deletes it for everyone. If you're culling hard, do it in your own library, not the one you share with Kim.
  • Recently Deleted holds everything for 30 days. Nothing you bin on the trip is final, so delete freely — but it also means the space isn't freed for a month.
  • A shared album is not a backup. Downsized copies. The backup is iCloud Photos, quietly, in the background.
  • Ship WiFi will choke on video. Big uploads want a hotel or a café on a port day. Let the phone hold them until then.
  • Menu names move between iOS versions. Apple reshuffles the Photos app most years — hunt for the feature by name (Favourites, Duplicates, Shared Album) rather than following an exact tap path.

If it gets away from you

If the nightly habit doesn't survive the first sea day — it happens — nothing is lost, it just moves the work to the Mac in September. Say the word when you're home and I'll do the sorting, de-duplicating and best-of pass with you on the laptop.
